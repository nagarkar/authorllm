# Ratified lenses — SMSTTD

Repo record of the lens artifacts ratified by the author (2026-08-31) and installed in `manuscripts/SMSTTD/_lenses/` (gitignored manuscript data — this file is the checked-in canonical copy, same pattern as the filter appendix in filter-pass-design.md). The live artifact is what runs; on any re-ratification update both.

## `metaphor-as-mechanism`

```markdown
# Metaphor presented as mechanism

You are looking for places where a figure — ledger, field, heartbeat,
gradient, ladder, current — is asked to do an argument's work: where a
conclusion rests on the metaphor's properties rather than on a defined term
or a stated premise.

Flag a passage when:
- a claim's only support IS the image (the ledger records, therefore nothing
  is lost);
- a metaphor's property is silently promoted to a law (fields exert force,
  so Choice "pulls");
- a term the book defines elsewhere is replaced by its image at exactly the
  point where precision would be tested.

Refuse to flag:
- illustration AFTER the argument — a figure decorating a conclusion already
  earned by defined terms;
- the Sermons' own register — the sermon voice speaks in figures by ratified
  design; this lens serves the essays and the Appendix;
- a figure the passage itself cashes out into the book's defined vocabulary.

For each finding, state what the metaphor is being trusted to prove, and
whether the book's own terms (Choice, Distinction, History, …) could carry
the claim without it.
```

## `repetition-across-parts`

```markdown
# Repetition across parts

You are looking for passages that re-teach what the reader has already been
taught — the same wall named and explained again, the same definition
re-derived, the same sermon re-summarized.

Flag a passage when:
- it re-explains a concept, metaphor, or definition an earlier essay (by
  reading order) has already established, in terms a reader of that essay
  gains nothing from;
- it re-summarizes a Sermon's argument the Recapitulation has already mapped,
  without adding a new consequence, objection, or application;
- it names the four walls again where the section's work does not need them.

Refuse to flag:
- a deliberate refrain — the book's motifs recur by design, and a refrain
  earns its place by rhythm, not information;
- a one-clause recall that ORIENTS ("as the essay on rebirth argued…") —
  orientation is a sentence, re-teaching is a paragraph;
- a re-statement that advances: a new consequence, a sharper formulation, or
  a different reader's door into the same room.

For each finding, name where the earlier telling lives (essay, by name) and
say what the flagged passage adds. If the honest answer is "nothing", say so
— that is the finding.
```

## `uncited-apparatus`

```markdown
# Uncited apparatus

You are looking for load-bearing borrowings that arrive without a source:
quotations, translations, etymologies, historical claims, scientific
results, and doctrinal attributions a specialist could check — and would.

Flag a passage when:
- it quotes or closely paraphrases a text (scripture, Jung, a named thinker)
  with no work or edition cited anywhere in the essay;
- it asserts an etymology, or a Sanskrit, Greek, or Latin meaning, as
  established fact;
- it attributes a doctrine to a tradition in a form a scholar of that
  tradition might dispute;
- it invokes a scientific result as settled support.

Refuse to flag:
- the author's own coinages and the book's internal vocabulary;
- common knowledge no specialist would demand a source for;
- passages already footnoted, unless the note cites nothing checkable.

For each finding, say which reader would check it (Sanskritist, historian of
Gnosticism, physicist, …) and what a sufficient citation looks like — a work
and a location, not "see the literature".
```


## `historical-reach` (ratified 2026-09-01)

The whole-reading half of a pair with the `modal-register` filter
(docs/filters.md). A single unhedged sentence is the filter's business; the
missing frame, the two law-claims that collide, and the mismatch between where
the essay hedges and where its evidence actually reaches are only visible from
here.

```markdown
# A model presented as history

You are looking for the gap between what an essay demonstrates and what its
prose claims: where a conceptual model — a pattern the essay proposes — is
presented as a pattern history has been shown to follow.

This is the whole-reading half of a pair. The sentence-by-sentence half is the
modal-register filter, and the division is not a convenience: a single
unhedged verb is that filter's business, and everything below can only be seen
by someone holding the essay entire.

Flag a passage when:

- **The architecture is demonstrated one way and claimed another.** The
  material establishes that several traditions share a structure; the prose
  concludes that societies have run a sequence. Agreement across traditions is
  evidence about the traditions. It is not, by itself, evidence that anything
  happened in the order proposed.
- **The model runs without a frame.** Nowhere does the essay say what kind of
  thing it is offering — a model, a tendency, a possibility, a law. Absent
  that sentence, the generic present is the only signal a reader has, and the
  generic present reads as report. The missing frame is a finding in its own
  right, and usually the cheapest one to close.
- **Two claims of the same shape point in opposite directions.** As tendencies
  they coexist without strain; as laws they collide. A collision is the
  strongest available evidence that the register is wrong, because it shows
  the law-reading producing an inconsistency the essay would not otherwise
  have. Name both passages.
- **The hedging and the evidence run in opposite directions.** Claims about
  individuals carefully qualified, claims about civilizations stated flat,
  where what the essay actually supports is the individual case. This
  inversion is common and it is diagnostic: the prose is most confident
  exactly where it is least supported.
- **A leg of the argument is asserted and never argued.** A stage of the
  proposed pattern that no example, no tradition, and no citation anywhere in
  the essay reaches. The essay may still want the stage. It has to earn it, or
  say that it has not.

Refuse to flag:

- **An essay that has declared its frame and then works inside it.** Once the
  kind of claim is on the record, the generic present is the correct tense and
  a page of it is not a finding.
- **A single unhedged sentence.** That is one unit's fault and it belongs to
  the filter. Report only what the whole reading shows.
- **An argument from the book's own definitions.** A conclusion drawn from
  defined terms is the book reasoning. Its scope is conceptual and it owes
  history nothing.
- **The Sermons.** Their register asserts by ratified design.
- **A claim the essay openly marks as speculation.** Where the essay has
  already conceded the reach, the concession is the answer to this lens and
  the passage is closed.

For each finding, state three things and stop:

1. **What the essay actually demonstrates** — the evidence class, named: cited
   texts, an argument from defined terms, a historical example, an analogy, or
   nothing.
2. **What the prose claims** — quoted, in the essay's own words.
3. **The smallest concession that closes the gap** — a declared frame, a
   narrowed scope, or a stated objection. Prefer the frame: one sentence at
   the head of a section can license everything under it, where narrowing each
   claim costs paragraphs.

Where the strongest objection to the essay simply *is* the gap you have found,
say so plainly. The ratified structure law asks every major essay to state its
strongest counterargument at full force with concession where real, and a gap
the essay concedes in its own voice is no longer a gap a reader can open
against it.
```
