# Clear sentences for a common reader

*The standard is the author's own edits to `god.md`, 2026-11-04. Same meaning, simpler language. The reader is the AUDIENCE PROFILE's common reader, not a professional philosopher.*

## Examples

### Flag — Plain wording exists
> not readily subject to measurement
Repair: "not easy to measure"

> The Field of Choice is immutable and beyond measurement.
Repair: "The Field of Choice never changes and cannot be measured."

### Flag — Long sentence with a weak start
> Whether the idol succeeds depends on the effect it has inside the one who attends to it.
Repair: "An idol succeeds only if it changes the person who attends to it."

> But what acts within us is not exhausted by what the conscious mind can presently recall.
Repair: "But more acts within us than we can remember."

### Flag — A gesture instead of the content
> It is roughly half right, and I have now conceded the half that is right, so the half that is wrong can be seen.
Why: the reader cannot say what was conceded. Repair: say it. "The iconoclast is half right. No image can contain the Field of Choice, and an image mistaken for the real thing misleads us."

### Flag — The conclusion does not follow from the reason
> Neither move happens on command, because both can taste like death.
Repair: supply the missing step. "Both moves can feel like dying, and we resist whatever feels like dying. So neither can be done by simply deciding to do it."

### Flag — A list that does not match its frame
> the priests, storytellers, ritual specialists, families, and institutions predisposed to preserving memory
Why: the frame calls for roles; families and institutions are not roles. Repair: "the priests, storytellers, ritual specialists, and family elders who preserve memory"

### Flag — The claim comes after its illustrations
A paragraph that opens "This practice … is a common theme" and only later says what the examples prove. Repair: lead with the claim.

### Keep — Plain and hard
> The paint, the stone, or the pixels remain paint, stone and pixels.
Keep the book's defined terms and its thinkers too: the author kept "Field of Choice", Wittgenstein and Gödel. He removed the difficulty around them, not them.

## The lens

Find sentences and short passages more complex than their meaning requires, and rewrite them so the common reader follows on one careful reading. **Meaning must not change.** A philosopher should agree the rewrite says the same thing. Flag when:

- **Plain wording exists.** An abstract, elevated or Latinate phrase where everyday words say the same thing.
- **Long sentence with a weak start.** A sentence that makes the reader hold too much, or opens on "whether", "it is", "what", or a nominalization. Break it up or lead with its subject.
- **A gesture instead of the content.** "As conceded", "this operation", "what remains" and the like, where the reader cannot say what is meant.
- **The conclusion does not follow from the reason.** A "because" or "therefore" with a missing step. Supply the step only if the essay supports it; otherwise it is a judgment.
- **A list that does not match its frame.**
- **The claim comes after its illustrations.**
- **A name or term does the work of an explanation.** A thinker, school or term the sentence fails without, under the AUDIENCE PROFILE. Add a plain clause, using the GLOSSARY or the essay's own words only. Never invent a definition of a term of the book. If neither supplies one, make it a judgment.
- **The reader has no way in.** A long abstract stretch with nothing to picture, or an opening that does not say what question the essay answers. These usually need the author, so they are judgments, with options.

Do not flag:

- **Genuine difficulty.** A hard idea stated as simply as it can be stays hard.
- **Precision.** A qualifier, scope or technical distinction the claim needs stays, even where it makes the sentence longer. When unsure whether a rewrite keeps the meaning, leave the sentence alone.
- **The book's defined terms** used as defined, or names the sentence works without.
- **Voice.** A sentence that is plain and has rhythm or force keeps it. Do not flatten images or cadence for the sake of simplicity.
- **A PROTECTED REGISTER,** a quotation from another author, or footnotes.

Keep footnote markers such as [^F2] in any replacement, and do not add new facts. Report every passage that qualifies, up to thirty for an essay, worst first. Short notes: what is hard, and what the rewrite does.
