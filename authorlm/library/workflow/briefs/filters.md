# How the filters work

Sent once, above the chosen filters, in the filter step's standing instructions.

1. Apply only the filters this turn names, in the order they are listed here, whatever order the turn names them in. Each works on the text as the filters before it left it.
2. Each concern has one owner. A filter changes only what its own rules name. It never undoes or reworks a change an earlier filter made in this reply, or text the author accepted earlier in this session. A fault that belongs to another filter is left as it stands.
3. The style rules outrank every filter: never make a change a style rule forbids. The author's decisions earlier in this session are context weighed with judgment: treat the same construction the same way unless good practice says otherwise, and then say why.
4. Lint owns what code can check and fix (quotation marks, dash form, spelling by dictionary, footnote anchors); no filter repeats it.
5. Leave untouched: headings, verse, footnote definitions, the words of another writer, tags in square brackets, [Omit] blocks, math and code. Keep every `[^…]` anchor at its sentence. Quoted speech and a told tale keep their idiom.
6. Key terms keep their exact form: the protected list, any word the essay defines, any word in the concept notes. A key term is never reworded, varied or recased, except where `cross-references` proposes the defined sense; elsewhere, recast the sentence around it, or ask.
7. Keep every claim, qualifier, example, story, image and step of reasoning unless a filter's rule says otherwise. Add no fact, source, date or example that the essay or the reference essays do not contain.
8. Repetition that is a technique stays: a refrain, anaphora, a parallel series, a key term kept constant, a build to a climax, a recall for a listener. When unsure whether a repetition is a tic or a refrain, it is a refrain.
9. Change a sentence only where a careful reader would stumble, misread or lose the thread; when unsure whether it is a fault, it is not. Read the new wording aloud in your head; if it is stiffer or harder to follow than the old, keep the old.
10. Propose the rewrite whenever the essay and the context supply what it needs. Where only the author knows (which noun a pronoun means, a missing fact, a choice between two claims), keep the sentence and plant `[Suggestion: Query: <one line naming both readings>]` after it. Ask a recurring question once, at its first place.
11. A unit with nothing to change passes unchanged. Mark every change with the name of the filter that made it, and give its reason in a few plain words before the new text.
