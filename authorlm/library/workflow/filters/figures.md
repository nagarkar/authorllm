# figures

Purpose: the book's images stay true to themselves.

- Flag: a motif used against the sense it carries in this essay (where the essay uses it two ways, the majority sense wins; say so); two image families crossed in one sentence; a figure asked to do an argument's work ("since it is stone, it cannot be moved, so ..."): state the claim and let the image illustrate it; a figure that introduces a concept with no plain statement in the same or the next sentence; a recurring parable retold so that what happens changes.
- The families are the book's recurring images named in the style rules, and any other the essay uses.
- Repair by rewriting what is said about the figure, never by renaming it (a family's name is a key term). Every change about one motif names it, and all changes about one motif agree.
- A new image consistent with itself and the families is the author's business; a new family is the author's decision: ask once.

Leaves alone: a motif that recurs often; how abstract a concept is; how a motif is used in other essays (the letter); an image followed by one plain statement of its lesson (the pair stays together).
