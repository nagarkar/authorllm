# copyedit

Purpose: faults with a right answer, grammar first, then marks. Each change shows on the unit's form, marked copyedit, so a unit of small fixes is one click. Never touch what lint owns: quotation marks, dash form, spelling by dictionary, footnote anchors, a doubled word.

- Grammar: a clause cut off by a full stop from the clause it needs (in narration, only one opened by because, although or which); a verb that disagrees with its subject ("a number of critics have" is right); a pronoun in the wrong case; an opening phrase a reader attaches to the wrong actor ("Walking home, the rain began"), never "given", "considering", "based on" and their kind; a modifier so placed that the sense goes astray; a series or paired construction in unlike forms; a tense that drifts (what a text says in the present, what its author did in the past, is right); a word confused with its twin (affect/effect, imply/infer, principle/principal, discreet/discrete, flout/flaunt).
- Marks: its/it's, their/there, then/than; a series comma against the convention; a parenthetic phrase with one comma; a comma before a joining "and" only where a reader would misread without it; a comma splice (a full stop, or a semicolon where the essay uses them); a colon between a verb and its object; drift from a settled convention (ellipsis, a hyphen in a compound before a noun, numbers, italics); a missing end mark, an unclosed bracket.
- Conventions: the style rules decide; where they are silent, the essay's settled form; where it is mixed, ask once: `[Suggestion: Query: style sheet: <convention>, n of one form and m of the other; which is the book's?]`.
- Never a fault: "which" or "that" either way, a split infinitive, a closing preposition, a sentence opening with And or But, singular "they", "hopefully", "this" for a whole clause, "due to", an omitted "that".
- Grammar: the fewest words that make it right. Marks: marks only; never reword to avoid a punctuation question.

Leaves alone: an optional comma that marks a pause; a chain of "and"s on purpose; whether a dash or a comma suits an interruption.
