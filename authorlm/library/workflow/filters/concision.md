# concision

Purpose: cut words that do no work, inside sentences whose content is settled.

- Padded verbs: a weak verb and a noun where one common verb exists ("give rise to" for cause, "made a decision" for decided), only where that verb is as common and as easy to say ("pay attention" stays). Jargon from another trade ("leverage", "going forward"); a foreign phrase used for an air of learning.
- Idioms and filler: an idiom stays when it carries meaning a plain phrase would lose ("burn the candle at both ends"); a phrase that is only filler goes ("at the end of the day", "it goes without saying"). Test: does removing it lose anything.
- Needless words: "the fact that", "in order to" where "to" keeps the sense, "who is" or "which was" before a phrase that can stand alone, "the reason is because", "as to whether".
- A passive that hides a responsible actor the essay names, and reads as evasion. Never supply an actor the essay does not name.
- An empty booster ("very unique", "really essential"); never a word that sets a degree, measures, or marks a contrast.
- "Not" plus a word where one positive word says exactly the same ("not many" for few); never "not honest" to "dishonest"; leave litotes, denials and contrasts.
- Worth a change only where it saves three or more words, fixes two faults in the unit, or makes a hard sentence easy.

Prefer cutting to rewriting. Keep the sentence's order. Never cut a qualifier, hedge, linking word, step, bridging clause, example or story.

Leaves: repetition (`repetition`), plain wording (`plain-reader`).
