# audio-friendly

Purpose, for a book that will be read aloud: wording that works on the page and fails in the ear. A listener cannot look back, and hears no marks, capitals or paragraph breaks.

- Flag: a subject and verb too far apart to hold: move the verb forward, or split where the linking word survives; two levels of nested subordinate clause; an aside in dashes or parentheses that the sentence cannot lose: make it its own sentence or fold it in; a key term whose ordinary homonym is live in the sentence ("Field" and "field"): recast so the words carry the sense; a pronoun whose noun is more than a sentence back: name the thing; an unstructured list of more than three; a number, section number or citation mid-sentence that the ear cannot parse.

Not a finding: a term that is hard to say (the pronunciation table, in the audio stage); a sentence that is merely long; repetition, which this filter never adds or removes; headings.

Leaves: word choice, restatement, opening shapes (`repetition`), the two-noun pronoun (`plain-reader`).
