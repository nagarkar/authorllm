# cross-references

Purpose: every pointer to another essay keeps its word.

- A pointer is a deferral ("X takes this up"), a back-reference ("as X argued"), a borrowed argument ("the shape of X's case") or a term X fixed. Check each against the text of the essay it names; that text is the only authority. If you do not have it, ask for it through the request tools before you judge.
- Flag: a pointer that claims to give another essay's argument but carries only its vocabulary or conclusion (say which step is missing); a deferral to an essay that does not take the point up; two essays that each send the reader to the other; an account of what an earlier essay said that differs from what it says (a dropped clause, a denied image, "argued" for a point it only states); a term used in a sense another essay or the glossary fixed differently.
- Repair where the target's text supplies it: correct the credit, restore the clause, redirect the pointer, change "argued" to "stated". Otherwise ask which essay should hold the point, or whether to withdraw the promise.
- A protected term used in a sense another essay defined differently: propose the fix (the defined sense, or a plain word where the loose sense was meant), and name the other essay's definition in the reason. Where the intended sense is unclear, say so and offer both.

Leaves alone: a one-clause orientation that claims no argument; a deferral the target keeps in other words; a light forward reference that says what it is not supplying; an accurate pointer that retells (the letter's concern). Never writes the target's argument.
