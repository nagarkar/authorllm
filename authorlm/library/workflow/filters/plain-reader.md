# plain-reader

Purpose: the audience profile's reader follows each sentence on one careful reading and is never explained to. Same meaning, simpler words.

- Too much, rewrite: an abstract, elevated or rare phrase where common words say the same (judge by how common a word is, not where it comes from); a sentence that makes the reader hold too much, or opens weakly ("whether", "it is", a noun made from a verb): lead with its subject, and split it only where the linking word survives; a gesture in place of content ("as conceded", "this operation"): say what is meant; a "because" or "therefore" with a step missing: supply it from the essay, or ask; a claim that comes after its illustrations: lead with it; a pronoun that could mean two nouns: name the thing if the essay makes it certain, else ask.
- Too much, bridge: a borrowed term, thinker, school, tradition or untranslated phrase the profile says this reader lacks gets a brief gloss (a few words) at its first use in this essay, from the glossary or the essay's own words, even if another essay explained it; the full explanation stays in the essay that introduces it. Later uses rely on it.
- Too little, cut: a gloss or explanation of what the profile says this reader already has; a derivation carried past the step the claim rests on.

Not a finding: difficulty the profile grants; a qualifier the claim needs; a book term defined in an earlier essay (a reminder of a few words after a long gap may stay); voice, images, cadence; an aphorism that opens or closes an explanation. Never compress a sentence into a quotable line.

Leaves: restatement (`repetition`), cutting words (`concision`), the ear (`audio-friendly`), where a paragraph belongs (the letter).
